No Thanks!

Pay a chip to pass, or take the card and the chips on it. Lowest score wins.

Rules and bots
  • Cards 3 to 35, 9 removed unseen, so 24 are played. Chips at the start: 11 each for 3 to 5 players, 9 for 6, 7 for 7.
  • Pass by paying a chip, or take the card and every chip on it. With no chips you must take it. Whoever takes a card turns over the next one.
  • In a run of consecutive numbers only the lowest card scores; the others are shown struck through. Each chip left is −1.
  • Opponents' chips are hidden until the end unless you tick the box.
  • Bots: "Rust pool" is the 49 strategies from your tournament file, ported to JavaScript (over 1M five-player games each bot's win rate matches the Rust program's within 0.5 points). "Mixed" seats one of three bots I wrote plus the Rust pool; "Strongest" seats my three plus PhasedBargain with opening limits 6 to 10, the best Rust bots.
  • My bots, in five-player games of about 500 to 4,500 each: Claude MonteCarlo(rollouts=150) won about 54% against four of the top five Rust bots (an equal share is 20%; PhasedBargain(9) gets about 24%, so it is clearly stronger). MonteCarlo(40) won about 42%. Claude Squeeze won about 24%, no better than PhasedBargain(9) it is built on. Monte Carlo plays by simulating the rest of the game from the unseen cards, and it aims to win, not to post a low score.
  • Rules checked against the published rules summary and the publisher's page.
